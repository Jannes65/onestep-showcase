The Pitch

A financial wellness app, one step at a time.

OneStep helps people build a complete picture of their money — not in a spreadsheet, not in a budget — but in 12 small daily steps. Insurance one day. Housing the next. Food the day after.

The problem

Most people don't know what they spend.

Budgeting apps demand a full setup on day one. Most people quit before they finish. OneStep flips it: complete one section per day, earn points and streaks, and get a real picture of your finances within two weeks — without the dread.

The solution

Guided, gamified, household-aware.

12 financial sections (rent, transport, insurance, debt…) collected step by step. Households can pool data. Bodyguard — the AI layer — coaches users to reduce costs, build a buffer, or invest the surplus. No advice, no products. Just clarity.

Why it matters

The Vault unlocks the business model.

Once a user uploads their actual policy or statement, OneStep can compare it, generate an anonymised lead package with consent, and route it to a supplier. Supplier pays a lead fee. A portion flows back to the consumer as rewards. Everyone wins.

Tab 02

Built with Claude, not just by Claude.

A real timeline of how OneStep grew from a Replit prototype into an AWS-bound product. AI did the heavy code lifting; product direction and judgement stayed with me.

Phase 01 · Foundation
12 sections + onboarding
React + Vite frontend, Express backend, Postgres + Drizzle. Mobile-first, max-width 448px. Zustand for state, shadcn/ui for components.
Phase 02 · Households
Aggregates API + scope toggle
Personal vs household view. Server-side aggregation in cents. Authorization keyed off session, not client. Regression tests for cross-user overwrites.
Phase 03 · Bodyguard AI
Three intents, structured responses
REDUCE_COSTS · BUILD_BUFFER · INVEST_ALLOCATE. Category-aware tips library (37+ categories). Strict guardrails: no income → "add income first" not generic advice.
Phase 04 · Vault
Uploads + Support Cases + consent
Store-first architecture: uploads succeed even if extraction fails. Support Cases bundle docs with explicit consent. Suppliers never browse the Vault — only see Case views.
Phase 05 · Migration
Replit → AWS (in progress)
Local file storage moves to S3. Replit-style trust proxy gets replaced with proper deployment config. Codebase audit for time-to-complete language (one intentional exception remains).
Phase 06 · Next
Proof Ledger → Credit → Rewards
Tag uploads as proof events. Surface credit score flags. Build improvement engine. Add behaviour scoring. Then — and only then — turn on rewards.

Tab 04

The flywheel.

OneStep's revenue model isn't ads, isn't subscriptions. It's a lead generation engine — measurement-first, consent-driven, with rewards flowing back to the consumer.

1
Bodyguard spots an expensive line item
"Your car insurance is 12% of monthly expenses. Want a better quote?"
2
User uploads existing policy to Vault
Store-first: works even if extraction fails. Module-safe fallback prompts otherwise.
3
Anonymised lead package generated
Minimum-safe dataset only: age band, area, product type, cost band, constraints. No raw doc. No personal info. Consent toggle required.
4
Submitted to supplier or broker
Suppliers never browse the Vault. They only see the Case view, scoped to what consent allows.
5
User sees comparison + recommendation
Side-by-side. Honest. No invented numbers, no product push.
6
User accepts → supplier pays lead fee
A portion flows to the consumer as rewards. The user gets paid for sharing their own data on their own terms.
Why this works

The consumer is the customer, not the product.

Most fintech monetises by selling user data or pushing products. OneStep monetises by lowering bills and sharing the upside. Trust compounds. So does the moat.

Sequencing matters. Rewards only turn on after Proof Ledger + behaviour scoring exist. Otherwise rewards become a gimmick. Measurement first, money second.
